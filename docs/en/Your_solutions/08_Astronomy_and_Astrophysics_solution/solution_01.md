# Problem 1 – Rotational Velocity of the Earth

## Problem Statement

Calculate the linear speed of a point on Earth's equator due to Earth's rotation.

Given:

$$
R_E = 6378\ \text{km}
$$

Earth completes one rotation in:

$$
T = 24\ \text{hours}
$$

Determine the linear speed in km/s.

---

## Theory

A point on the equator travels one full circumference during one rotation.

The circumference of a circle is:

$$
C = 2\pi R
$$

The linear speed is:

$$
v = \frac{C}{T}
$$

where:

- $v$ = linear speed
- $C$ = circumference
- $R$ = Earth's radius
- $T$ = rotation period

---

## Step 1: Calculate Circumference

Using:

$$
C = 2\pi R
$$

Substitute:

$$
C = 2\pi(6378)
$$

$$
C = 40074\ \text{km}
$$

---

## Step 2: Convert Time to Seconds

One day:

$$
T = 24 \times 3600
$$

$$
T = 86400\ \text{s}
$$

---

## Step 3: Calculate Speed

$$
v = \frac{40074}{86400}
$$

$$
v = 0.464\ \text{km/s}
$$

---

## Final Answer

$$
\boxed{v \approx 0.464\ \text{km/s}}
$$

or

$$
\boxed{v \approx 464\ \text{m/s}}
$$

---

## Interpretation

A person standing at the equator is moving at approximately

$$
0.464\ \text{km/s}
$$

due to Earth's rotation, even though they do not feel this motion because everything around them is moving at the same speed.

---

## Presentation Notes

### Meaning of Symbols

- $R_E$ = Earth's radius
- $C$ = circumference
- $T$ = rotation period
- $v$ = linear speed
- $\pi \approx 3.1416$

### What to Say

> Earth rotates once every 24 hours. A person at the equator travels one complete circumference during this time. First, we calculate Earth's circumference using $2\pi R$. Then we divide the circumference by the rotation period. This gives a speed of approximately 0.464 km/s, which means a point on the equator moves at about 464 meters per second due to Earth's rotation.
