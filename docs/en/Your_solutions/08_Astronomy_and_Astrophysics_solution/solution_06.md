# Problem 6 – Solar Gravity

## Problem Statement

Calculate the acceleration due to gravity on the surface of the Sun.

Determine:

1. The gravitational acceleration on the Sun's surface.
2. By what factor your weight would increase if you could stand on the Sun.

---

# Given Data

Mass of the Sun:

$$
M_S = 2.0\times10^{30}\ \text{kg}
$$

Radius of the Sun:

$$
R_S = 6.96\times10^8\ \text{m}
$$

Gravitational constant:

$$
G = 6.674\times10^{-11}
\ \text{N m}^2/\text{kg}^2
$$

Gravity on Earth:

$$
g_E = 9.81\ \text{m/s}^2
$$

---

# Theory

The gravitational acceleration at the surface of a celestial body is:

$$
g=\frac{GM}{R^2}
$$

where:

- $g$ = gravitational acceleration
- $G$ = gravitational constant
- $M$ = mass of the body
- $R$ = radius of the body

---

# Step 1: Write the Formula

For the Sun:

$$
g_S=
\frac{
GM_S
}{
R_S^2
}
$$

Substitute values:

$$
g_S=
\frac{
(6.674\times10^{-11})
(2.0\times10^{30})
}
{
(6.96\times10^8)^2
}
$$

---

# Step 2: Calculate the Numerator

$$
GM_S=
(6.674\times10^{-11})
(2.0\times10^{30})
$$

Multiply the numbers:

$$
6.674\times2.0
=
13.348
$$

Multiply the powers of ten:

$$
10^{-11}\times10^{30}
=
10^{19}
$$

Therefore:

$$
GM_S=
13.348\times10^{19}
$$

Rewriting:

$$
GM_S=
1.3348\times10^{20}
$$

---

# Step 3: Calculate the Denominator

$$
R_S^2
=
(6.96\times10^8)^2
$$

Square the number:

$$
6.96^2
=
48.44
$$

Square the power:

$$
(10^8)^2
=
10^{16}
$$

Therefore:

$$
R_S^2
=
48.44\times10^{16}
$$

Rewriting:

$$
R_S^2
=
4.844\times10^{17}
$$

---

# Step 4: Calculate Solar Gravity

$$
g_S=
\frac{
1.3348\times10^{20}
}
{
4.844\times10^{17}
}
$$

Divide the numbers:

$$
\frac{1.3348}{4.844}
=
0.2755
$$

Subtract powers:

$$
10^{20-17}
=
10^3
$$

Therefore:

$$
g_S=
0.2755\times10^3
$$

$$
g_S=
275.5\ \text{m/s}^2
$$

---

# Gravity on the Sun

$$
\boxed{
g_S \approx 275\ \text{m/s}^2
}
$$

---

# Step 5: Compare with Earth

Earth gravity:

$$
g_E=9.81\ \text{m/s}^2
$$

Calculate the factor:

$$
\frac{g_S}{g_E}
=
\frac{275.5}{9.81}
$$

$$
=
28.1
$$

---

# Weight Increase Factor

$$
\boxed{
28.1
}
$$

Your weight would be approximately 28 times larger on the Sun than on Earth.

---

# Example

Suppose a person has a mass of:

$$
m=70\ \text{kg}
$$

### Weight on Earth

$$
W_E=mg
$$

$$
W_E=(70)(9.81)
$$

$$
W_E=687\ \text{N}
$$

### Weight on the Sun

$$
W_S=(70)(275.5)
$$

$$
W_S=19285\ \text{N}
$$

Thus the same person would weigh:

$$
\boxed{
19285\ \text{N}
}
$$

on the Sun.

---

# Final Answers

### Gravity on the Sun

$$
\boxed{
g_S = 275\ \text{m/s}^2
}
$$

### Weight Increase Factor

$$
\boxed{
28.1
}
$$

A person would weigh approximately:

$$
\boxed{
28 \text{ times more}
}
$$

than on Earth.

---

# Physical Interpretation

The Sun is enormously massive.

Although it is much larger than Earth, its mass is so great that the gravitational pull at its surface is much stronger.

As a result:

- Objects fall much faster.
- Weight becomes much larger.
- A person would feel about 28 times heavier.

# Conclusion

The gravitational acceleration on the Sun's surface is approximately:

$$
275\ \text{m/s}^2
$$

which is about:

$$
28
$$

times larger than Earth's gravity.

Therefore, if a person could stand on the Sun, they would weigh approximately 28 times more than they do on Earth.
