# Problem 5 – Escape Velocity from the Moon

## Problem Statement

Determine the escape velocity from the surface of the Moon.

Given:

Moon's mass:

$$
M_M = 7.35\times10^{22}\ \text{kg}
$$

Moon's radius:

$$
R_M = 1737\ \text{km}
$$

Earth's escape velocity:

$$
v_E = 11.2\ \text{km/s}
$$

Calculate:

1. The escape velocity from the Moon.
2. Express the result in km/s.
3. Express the result as a fraction of Earth's escape velocity.

---

# Theory

Escape velocity is the minimum speed required for an object to completely escape the gravitational pull of a celestial body without additional propulsion.

The escape velocity formula is:

$$
v_e=\sqrt{\frac{2GM}{R}}
$$

where:

- $v_e$ = escape velocity (m/s)
- $G$ = gravitational constant
- $M$ = mass of the celestial body
- $R$ = radius of the celestial body

---

# Given Values

Gravitational constant:

$$
G = 6.674\times10^{-11}
\ \text{N m}^2/\text{kg}^2
$$

Moon's mass:

$$
M_M = 7.35\times10^{22}\ \text{kg}
$$

Moon's radius:

$$
R_M = 1737\ \text{km}
$$

---

# Step 1: Convert Radius to Meters

The formula requires SI units.

Since:

$$
1\ \text{km}=1000\ \text{m}
$$

Convert:

$$
R_M=1737\times1000
$$

$$
R_M=1.737\times10^6\ \text{m}
$$

---

# Step 2: Write the Escape Velocity Formula

$$
v_e=\sqrt{\frac{2GM_M}{R_M}}
$$

Substitute the values:

$$
v_e=
\sqrt{
\frac{
2(6.674\times10^{-11})
(7.35\times10^{22})
}
{
1.737\times10^6
}
}
$$

---

# Step 3: Calculate the Numerator

First calculate:

$$
2\times6.674\times7.35
$$

$$
=98.1078
$$

Now calculate the powers of ten:

$$
10^{-11}\times10^{22}
=
10^{11}
$$

Therefore:

$$
2GM_M
=
98.1078\times10^{11}
$$

Rewriting in scientific notation:

$$
2GM_M
=
9.81078\times10^{12}
$$

---

# Step 4: Divide by the Radius

$$
\frac{
9.81078\times10^{12}
}
{
1.737\times10^6
}
$$

$$
=
5.648\times10^6
$$

Therefore:

$$
v_e=
\sqrt{
5.648\times10^6
}
$$

---

# Step 5: Calculate the Square Root

$$
v_e
=
2376\ \text{m/s}
$$

Convert to kilometers per second:

$$
v_e
=
\frac{2376}{1000}
$$

$$
v_e
=
2.376\ \text{km/s}
$$

---

# Escape Velocity of the Moon

$$
\boxed{
v_e \approx 2.38\ \text{km/s}
}
$$

---

# Step 6: Compare with Earth's Escape Velocity

Earth's escape velocity:

$$
v_E = 11.2\ \text{km/s}
$$

Calculate the ratio:

$$
\frac{v_M}{v_E}
=
\frac{2.38}{11.2}
$$

$$
=
0.2125
$$

Convert to percentage:

$$
0.2125\times100
=
21.25\%
$$

---

# Final Answers

### Escape Velocity from the Moon

$$
\boxed{
v_M=2.38\ \text{km/s}
}
$$

### Fraction of Earth's Escape Velocity

$$
\boxed{
\frac{v_M}{v_E}=0.2125
}
$$

### Percentage of Earth's Escape Velocity

$$
\boxed{
21.25\%
}
$$

---

# Physical Interpretation

The Moon has much less mass than Earth and therefore exerts a weaker gravitational force.

Because of this weaker gravity:

- Less speed is needed to escape.
- Rockets require less fuel to launch from the Moon.
- The escape velocity is much lower than Earth's.

The Moon's escape velocity is only about one-fifth of Earth's escape velocity.

---

---

# Conclusion

The escape velocity from the Moon is:

$$
2.38\ \text{km/s}
$$

This is only:

$$
21.25\%
$$

of Earth's escape velocity.

This demonstrates that the Moon's gravitational field is significantly weaker than Earth's, making it much easier for spacecraft to leave the Moon's surface.
