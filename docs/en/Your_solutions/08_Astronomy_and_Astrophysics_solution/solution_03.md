# Problem 3 – Microgravity at the ISS

## Problem Statement

The International Space Station (ISS) orbits Earth at an altitude of:

$$
h = 400\ \text{km}
$$

Determine:

1. The acceleration due to gravity at the ISS altitude.
2. Why astronauts experience weightlessness despite the presence of gravity.

---

## Given Data

Earth's radius:

$$
R_E = 6378\ \text{km}
$$

ISS altitude:

$$
h = 400\ \text{km}
$$

Earth's mass:

$$
M_E = 5.97\times10^{24}\ \text{kg}
$$

Gravitational constant:

$$
G = 6.674\times10^{-11}
\ \text{N m}^2/\text{kg}^2
$$

---

# Theory

The gravitational acceleration at a distance $r$ from Earth's center is:

$$
g=\frac{GM_E}{r^2}
$$

where:

- $g$ = gravitational acceleration
- $G$ = gravitational constant
- $M_E$ = Earth's mass
- $r$ = distance from Earth's center

---

# Step 1: Calculate Distance from Earth's Center

The ISS is located:

$$
400\ \text{km}
$$

above Earth's surface.

Therefore:

$$
r=R_E+h
$$

Substitute values:

$$
r=6378+400
$$

$$
r=6778\ \text{km}
$$

Convert to meters:

$$
r=6.778\times10^6\ \text{m}
$$

---

# Step 2: Apply Gravitational Formula

$$
g=
\frac{
GM_E
}{
r^2
}
$$

Substitute values:

$$
g=
\frac{
(6.674\times10^{-11})
(5.97\times10^{24})
}{
(6.778\times10^6)^2
}
$$

---

# Step 3: Calculate Numerator

$$
GM_E
=
3.986\times10^{14}
$$

---

# Step 4: Calculate Denominator

$$
r^2
=
4.594\times10^{13}
$$

---

# Step 5: Calculate Gravity

$$
g=
\frac{
3.986\times10^{14}
}{
4.594\times10^{13}
}
$$

$$
g=8.68\ \text{m/s}^2
$$

---

# Final Result

The gravitational acceleration at ISS altitude is:

$$
\boxed{
g \approx 8.68\ \text{m/s}^2
}
$$

---

# Comparison with Earth's Surface

At Earth's surface:

$$
g_0=9.81\ \text{m/s}^2
$$

At ISS altitude:

$$
g=8.68\ \text{m/s}^2
$$

Ratio:

$$
\frac{8.68}{9.81}
=
0.885
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
g_{ISS}
\approx 89\%
\text{ of surface gravity}
}
$$

---

# Why Do Astronauts Feel Weightless?

Many people think there is no gravity in space.

This is not true.

The ISS still experiences almost:

$$
89\%
$$

of Earth's gravity.

---

## The Real Reason

The ISS and astronauts are both falling toward Earth at exactly the same rate.

This is called:

### Free Fall

The ISS continuously falls toward Earth but moves forward so quickly that it keeps missing Earth.

As a result:

- The station falls.
- The astronauts fall.
- Everything inside falls together.

Because there is no supporting floor force pushing upward, astronauts feel weightless.

---

# Physical Interpretation

Astronauts are not floating because gravity disappeared.

They are floating because:

$$
\boxed{
\text{everything is falling together}
}
$$

inside the orbiting spacecraft.

# Final Answers

### Gravity at ISS Altitude

$$
\boxed{
g \approx 8.68\ \text{m/s}^2
}
$$

---

### Why Astronauts Feel Weightless

$$
\boxed{
\text{Because they are in continuous free fall around Earth}
}
$$

>
